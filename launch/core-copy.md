## What changed in V2 (for "what's new" posts)

- **Decision memo replaces the decision brief.** Sections: what's working, hidden risks, objections, path forward, fixes to try, exhibits (the reactions behind each claim).
- **Calibrated tone.** No flattery, no catastrophizing, always a next step. Checked by an eval suite with trap cases: flattery bait, harshness bait, and pitches with a planted hidden risk the memo must catch.
- **Three review types.** Validate (does the market care?), Investor (would a partner take the meeting? deck upload supported) and Launch (how will HN, PH and Reddit react on launch day? verdict: go / sharpen / hold).
- **Pricing.** Small reviews stay free, unlimited, no signup. Packs unlock 500-reviewer panels, Investor and Launch reviews, deck upload, PDF export and share cards.
- **Memo archive** on the home page, and a new domain: swarmie.harshpatadia.com.

## One-Paragraph Pitch

Swarmie convenes a panel of AI reviewers (20 on the free tier, up to 500 with a pack) and runs your pitch past them. Each reviewer has its own background, bias and attention span, and part of the panel scrolls past without a word, because real audiences do. In about a minute you get a **decision memo**: a verdict, what's working, the hidden risks, the objections with the reactions behind them, and a path forward with fixes to try this week. Choose a Validate, Investor or Launch review depending on what you're about to do. Small reviews are free with no signup. It's a pre-interview filter, not research. Open source under AGPL-3.0, and free to self-host on local Ollama.

## Anti-Wrapper Paragraph

Every idea validator eventually hands you a number. "7.4 / 10." "89% market fit." It means nothing and costs nothing to ignore. Swarmie hands you a memo instead: the specific risk you haven't asked a real user about yet, the reactions that raised it, and the test to run this week. V2 is also tuned not to flatter you and not to bury you. It names what's working, then names what isn't.

## What Swarmie Is NOT

- **Not a prediction engine.** It does not tell you whether your startup will succeed.
- **Not a research replacement.** Synthetic reactions are not user interviews. They are a pre-filter.
- **Not a score.** There is a verdict and a next step, not a 7.4/10.
- **Not accurate by definition.** AI reviewers carry the biases of their training data. They can be wrong. We say so, loudly.
- **Not market validation.** A good memo is a reason to talk to real humans sooner, not a reason to skip them.

## Review Types (supplemental positioning)

- **Validate** (free, or up to 500 reviewers with a pack): does the market care? Verdict: ship it / sharpen / wrong audience / rethink the core.
- **Investor** (pack): the questions a partner will likely ask in a first meeting and the missing proof points. Upload your deck. Verdict: fundable / sharpen story / wrong stage / not fundable.
- **Launch** (pack): how startup communities will react on launch day. Verdict: go / sharpen / hold.

Same rules for all three: synthetic, disclosed, and built to surface the gap before you're in the room, not to replace the room.

## The "Isn't This Just ChatGPT?" Answer

*Partly, yes, and the honest answer matters.*

Ask a single LLM "does this idea work?" and it averages everything into mush. It wants to be helpful, so it hedges. Swarmie makes up to 500 reviewers react independently, each with different biases, skepticism and reasons to scroll past. Silence is modelled on purpose: reviewers who ignore the pitch cost nothing and say nothing, like real audiences. The memo then cites the reactions behind every claim, so you can check its work. We test the memo against a fixed set of trap pitches every time we change it. And it's disclosed as synthetic from the first pixel. We never call it research.
